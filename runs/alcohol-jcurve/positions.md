# Position-space

## P1: Classic cardioprotection (the real J-curve)
holder: observational-cohort tradition — Marmot, Klatsky, Rimm/Mukamal; meta-analyses Di Castelnuovo 2006, Ronksley 2011 (BMJ), Brien 2011 (BMJ)
claim: Moderate drinking (~1–2 drinks/day) causally lowers coronary heart disease and all-cause mortality versus abstention, and this benefit is real, not spurious.
rests_on: Reproducible J/U curves across dozens of prospective cohorts with dose-response, plus mechanism — ethanol raises HDL and adiponectin and lowers fibrinogen/platelet aggregation and insulin resistance in randomized feeding/biomarker trials.

## P2: Abstainer / sick-quitter bias (the artefact camp)
holder: Shaper 1988 (Lancet); Fillmore et al. 2006–07; Stockwell, Naimi, Zhao et al. 2016 (J Stud Alcohol Drugs), 2023 (JAMA Netw Open)
claim: The J-curve is manufactured by a contaminated reference group — "abstainers" bundle former drinkers and the already-sick who quit — and vanishes in studies that isolate lifelong abstainers.
rests_on: Reclassification meta-analyses where protection shrinks to null once occasional/former drinkers are separated out (only ~13 of 87 studies "bias-free," and those show no significant benefit), plus systematic confounding by healthier drinker socioeconomics.

## P3: Mendelian randomization — no CV protection, harm rises with dose
holder: Holmes et al. 2014 (BMJ, ADH1B/InterAct); Millwood et al. 2019 (Lancet, China Kadoorie Biobank, ALDH2/ADH1B)
claim: Genetically instrumented lifelong-lower alcohol lowers blood pressure and stroke roughly log-linearly with no protective threshold, so the observational U-shape is confounding rather than causation.
rests_on: Genetic variants as unconfounded, reverse-causation-proof exposure instruments; carriers who drink less have lower BP, stroke and CHD risk — implying reduction benefits even current moderate drinkers.

## P4: Pooled IPD / GBD — no safe level for TOTAL health
holder: GBD 2016 Alcohol Collaborators / Griswold et al. 2018 (Lancet); Wood et al. 2018 (Lancet, ERFC/EPIC-CVD/UK Biobank, ~600,000 drinkers)
claim: Summed across all 20+ alcohol-attributable conditions (cancers, injury, TB, liver), the intake minimizing total harm is zero and total mortality rises with dose, even where CVD alone shows a nadir.
rests_on: Large pooled individual-participant and DALY-weighted global data; Wood placed lowest all-cause-mortality risk below ~100 g/week and found rising stroke, heart failure, fatal hypertensive disease and aortic aneurysm with intake.

## P5: Residual low-dose benefit is real; all-cause ≠ CVD-specific
holder: Mukamal, Rimm; commentary critics of "no safe level" framing (e.g. Spiegelhalter); some GBD correspondence
claim: A genuine small reduction in ischaemic heart disease and diabetes survives bias correction at low doses, and the excess harm at ~1 drink/day is so tiny that "no safe level" overstates a near-flat curve.
rests_on: Wood 2018's own inverse association of alcohol with non-fatal MI, robust CHD/diabetes protective signals, and the point that the modelled low-dose risk increment is negligible in absolute terms (~1 extra case per tens of thousands of person-years).

## P6: It's the pattern/beverage, not the ethanol dose
holder: Renaud & de Lorgeril 1992 (Lancet, "French paradox"); Mukamal 2003 (NEJM, drinking frequency); binge-harm literature
claim: The mortality curve tracks *how* people drink — regular moderate intake with meals and wine polyphenols within a Mediterranean pattern — while binge/irregular drinking is harmful, so "dose" is the wrong axis.
rests_on: Frequency and beverage-type gradients (steady wine drinkers fare best, bingers worst at equal volume), confounded by diet/lifestyle patterns that dose-only models cannot separate from ethanol itself.

## STRONGEST-OBJECTION
claim: The thesis conflates total/all-cause harm with cardiovascular risk — for the specific ischaemic-heart-disease endpoint a genuine inverse association persists in exactly the bias-corrected pooled data the thesis relies on (Wood 2018's lower non-fatal MI), and MR does not refute it: Millwood 2019 found clear causal harm for stroke and BP but was underpowered and inconclusive for IHD, so coronary protection is not established to be purely artefactual.
rests_on: For the thesis to hold, low-dose ischaemic-heart-disease risk must be flat-or-rising and causal-null after abstainer-bias correction; it is wrong if a real, dose-dependent, mechanism-supported (HDL-mediated) reduction in coronary/MI risk survives that correction and remains compatible with — not excluded by — the genetic evidence.
