# Position-space (smoke fixture)

## P1: Automation-displacement
holder: labor economists (Acemoglu camp)
claim: AI substitutes for labor faster than it complements it, net-destroying jobs.
rests_on: task-exposure studies showing routine-task decline.

## P2: Augmentation-complementarity
holder: productivity optimists (Autor camp)
claim: AI complements workers, raising output per worker and creating new task demand.
rests_on: historical general-purpose-technology diffusion curves.

## P3: Distributional-not-level
holder: inequality scholars
claim: the level of employment barely moves; the composition and wage share shift.
rests_on: polarization evidence across the wage distribution.

## P4: Post-work-abundance
holder: post-scarcity futurists
claim: AI ends wage labor entirely; the right frame is redistribution, not employment.
rests_on: long-run extrapolation of automation to near-total task coverage.

## STRONGEST-OBJECTION
claim: it is too early to measure — AI's labor effect has not shown up in aggregate data.
rests_on: no break in aggregate productivity or unemployment series yet.
