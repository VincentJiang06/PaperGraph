# Position-space

## P1: Per-TWh mortality + lifecycle-emissions consensus
holder: Markandya & Wilkinson (Lancet 2007); Our World in Data / Hannah Ritchie; Sovacool et al.; IPCC AR5 harmonized LCA
claim: Measured over its operating history, nuclear's deaths per TWh (~0.03) and lifecycle emissions (~12 gCO2eq/kWh) are two to three orders of magnitude below coal/oil/gas and statistically indistinguishable from wind and solar.
rests_on: Comparative lifecycle assessments and death-per-TWh accounting that combine chronic air-pollution mortality with accident tolls across the full fuel chain.

## P2: Ecomodernist pro-nuclear necessity
holder: Hansen, Kharecha, Emanuel & Caldeira; Breakthrough Institute; Shellenberger
claim: Nuclear is not merely comparably safe but has already prevented ~1.8M air-pollution deaths and ~64 Gt CO2, and no historical decarbonization has matched the pace of the French/Swedish nuclear buildouts, so deep decarbonization requires expanding it.
rests_on: Kharecha & Hansen (2013) prevented-death/CO2 accounting, national decarbonization-rate comparisons, and energy-density/land-use arguments.

## P3: Catastrophic tail-risk / normal-accidents view
holder: Charles Perrow (Normal Accidents); Wheatley, Sornette & Sovacool (heavy-tailed accident statistics); post-Fukushima risk framing; Taleb-style precaution
claim: Point-estimate means understate nuclear risk because accident severity is heavy-tailed and tightly-coupled complex systems make catastrophic failure inherent, so expected harm is dominated by rare extreme events, latent cancers, and evacuation/psychosocial deaths that per-kWh averages omit.
rests_on: Power-law fits to the accident record, contested Chernobyl/Fukushima consequence estimates (TORCH/Yablokov vs UNSCEAR), and the sociology of tightly-coupled systems.

## P4: Anti-nuclear economics / soft-energy opportunity cost
holder: Amory Lovins / Rocky Mountain Institute
claim: Even granting that nuclear is safe, it is the wrong climate choice because it delivers less CO2 abatement per dollar and per year than efficiency plus renewables, so funding it actively slows decarbonization.
rests_on: LCOE trends (Lazard), chronic construction cost overruns and delays (Vogtle, Flamanville, Hinkley Point C, Olkiluoto), and nuclear's negative learning curve against falling solar/wind/battery costs.

## P5: 100% renewables feasible without nuclear
holder: Mark Jacobson (Wind-Water-Solar roadmaps); Stanford WWS group
claim: A fully renewable grid is technically and economically achievable without nuclear, whose meltdown risk, weapons linkage, and opportunity cost make it both unnecessary and undesirable.
rests_on: Jacobson & Delucchi continental WWS modeling and projected renewable-plus-storage cost declines.

## P6: Waste-longevity + proliferation intergenerational harm
holder: Frank von Hippel and nonproliferation scholars; geologic-repository critics; anti-nuclear environmental groups
claim: Nuclear's distinctive harm is not ordinary mortality but intergenerational and geopolitical — high-level waste stays hazardous for 10^4–10^5 years with no operating permanent repository, while the fuel cycle spreads weapons-usable material and know-how.
rests_on: The absence of operating deep repositories (Yucca cancelled; only Onkalo nearing operation) and documented civilian-to-weapons proliferation pathways (India, plutonium reprocessing).

## P7: Firm-power / system-cost counterpoint
holder: Jesse Jenkins / MIT "Future of Nuclear"; Clack et al. (2017 PNAS critique of Jacobson); Sepulveda et al.
claim: Variable renewables incur steeply rising storage, transmission, and integration costs at high penetration, so bare-LCOE comparisons overstate their substitutability and a low-cost deep-decarbonized grid still needs firm clean power like nuclear.
rests_on: Capacity-expansion modeling showing decarbonization costs rise sharply without firm low-carbon sources, and the Clack et al. rebuttal of 100%-renewable feasibility claims.

## STRONGEST-OBJECTION
claim: The thesis's clean split between nuclear's "ordinary realized mortality" and its "catastrophic tail-risk" is illegitimate for nuclear specifically, because its harm distribution is heavy-tailed and its one severe realized event has a latent-cancer toll that ranges from ~4,000 (UNSCEAR) to tens of thousands (TORCH/Fairlie) to ~1M (Yablokov) — so the low mean deaths/TWh is an artifact of a short, lucky observation window and contested undercounting, not a stable safety property that can be cleanly separated from tail risk.
rests_on: For the thesis to be wrong, true expected nuclear mortality — properly integrating the fat tail, LNT latent cancers, evacuation/exclusion-zone and psychosocial deaths, and the correct catastrophic-accident frequency — would have to be materially higher than the consensus point estimates, making "safety record" and "tail risk" inseparable rather than distinct grounds.
