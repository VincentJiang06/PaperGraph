# Topic
The Black Death and the divergent fate of serfdom in late-medieval Europe (the Brenner Debate).

# Core Question
Why did the same post-plague demographic collapse dissolve serfdom in Western Europe but entrench a "second serfdom" in Eastern Europe?

# Intended Thesis
In Western Europe the dissolution of serfdom after the Black Death (c. 1348–1500) was driven
primarily by the demographic collapse that raised the scarcity and bargaining power of peasant
labour; but because the identical demographic shock entrenched a second serfdom in Eastern
Europe, the divergent outcomes show demography operated only through pre-existing balances of
class and political power, not as an autonomous cause.

# Field Weight
humanities

# Scope
- Period: 1348–1600
- Region: Western Europe (England, France) vs East-Elbian Europe (Poland, Bohemia, eastern Germany)
- Mechanisms: demographic shock, factor-price (land/labour) shifts, lord–peasant class power,
  commercialization, state and legal institutions

# Exclusions
- Do not extrapolate to non-European agrarian systems.
- Do not treat "feudalism" as a single uniform institution across regions.
