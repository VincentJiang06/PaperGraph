# Topic
Is there a cardioprotective "safe" level of alcohol, or is the J-curve a confounding artefact?

# Core Question
Does moderate alcohol consumption genuinely lower cardiovascular/all-cause mortality, or does
the apparent benefit disappear once abstainer-bias and confounding are corrected?

# Intended Thesis
The apparent cardiovascular benefit of moderate drinking (the "J-curve") is largely an artefact
of confounding and abstainer (sick-quitter) bias; once these are addressed with genetic
(Mendelian-randomization) and pooled individual-participant analyses, there is no cardioprotective
threshold and total harm rises roughly monotonically with intake, so from a population-health
standpoint there is no risk-free level of alcohol.

# Field Weight
mixed

# Scope
- Adult populations; all-cause and cardiovascular mortality
- Study designs: observational cohorts, meta-analyses, Mendelian randomization
- Mechanisms: abstainer/sick-quitter bias, residual confounding, HDL/plausible biology

# Exclusions
- Do not make individual clinical dosing recommendations.
- Do not extend to alcohol-use-disorder treatment policy.

# Note
Mixed: strong interpretive divergence (does the benefit survive de-confounding?) AND empirical
rigor (study findings must verify verbatim; where a public dataset exists, reproduce a number).
