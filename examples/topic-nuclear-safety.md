# Topic
The comparative safety and carbon intensity of nuclear electricity versus fossil fuels.

# Core Question
Per unit of electricity generated, how do nuclear power's realized death rate and lifecycle
greenhouse-gas emissions compare with fossil fuels and with wind and solar?

# Intended Thesis
Per unit of electricity generated, nuclear power causes far fewer deaths and far lower
lifecycle greenhouse-gas emissions than fossil fuels — comparable to wind and solar — so on
realized-mortality and carbon grounds the objection to nuclear rests on catastrophic tail-risk,
waste longevity, cost, and proliferation, not on its ordinary safety or climate record.

# Field Weight
science

# Scope
- Comparison across generation sources: coal, oil, gas, nuclear, hydro, wind, solar
- Metrics: deaths per TWh (accidents + air pollution), lifecycle gCO2eq per kWh
- Global; modern fleet

# Exclusions
- Do not resolve levelized-cost / economics disputes (out of scope; note them).
- Do not adjudicate long-run waste-repository safety beyond citing positions.
