# Entry-level / recent-grad labor data 2025 (macro-confound bundle)
URLs:

- https://www.forbes.com/sites/garthfriesen/2025/10/23/unemployment-crisis-why-college-graduates-are-struggling-in-2025/

- https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/24/ai-was-supposed-to-kill-engineering-jobs-but-new-data-suggests-theyre-the-most-resilient/

KEY FIGURES (as reported):
- Young college grads (20-24, BA+) unemployment ~9.5% by Sep 2025, ~2x the general adult rate; NY Fed recent-grad measure ~5.7% end-2025. Experienced college workers stayed ~2-3%.
- Share of postings listed "entry-level" ~45% below the 5-year average in Q1 2025.
- Indeed: software-engineering listings fell to ~65% of Feb 2020 levels; ~3.5x fewer than the mid-2022 peak.

COUNTER-EVIDENCE (macro / not-AI):
- Big-tech total hiring fell ~25% vs 2019, but ENGINEERING roles fell only ~11%; engineers = 55% of new hires in 2025 (TechCrunch: engineering "most resilient").
- Entry-level decline began pre-ChatGPT and tracks rate hikes + post-pandemic tech over-hiring correction (see s08 Brookings first-inning).
=> the entry-level softness is REAL but jointly explained by AI + macro; cannot cleanly attribute.
