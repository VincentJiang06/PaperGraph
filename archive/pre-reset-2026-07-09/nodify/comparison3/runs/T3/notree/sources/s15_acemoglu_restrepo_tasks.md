# Acemoglu & Restrepo — "Automation and New Tasks: How Technology Displaces and Reinstates Labor" (JEP 2019; NBER 25684)
URL: https://www.nber.org/papers/w25684

CORE FRAMEWORK (as summarized from the paper):
- DISPLACEMENT effect: "automation ... enables capital to replace labor in tasks it was previously engaged in, shifts the task content of production against labor ... may reduce labor demand even as it raises productivity."
- REINSTATEMENT effect: "The introduction of new tasks changes the task content of production in favor of labor ... and always raises the labor share and labor demand."
- "So-so automation": "Some automation technologies may in fact reduce labor demand because they bring sizable displacement effects but modest productivity gains."

Relevance: the theoretical bridge between TASK-level substitution and JOB-level net change. Net employment = displacement (tasks lost) MINUS reinstatement (new tasks created) PLUS productivity/scale effects. The sign is theoretically ambiguous and depends on whether AI is "so-so" or genuinely productive, and on how fast new tasks appear.
