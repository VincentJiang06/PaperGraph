# Acemoglu — "The Simple Macroeconomics of AI" (NBER WP 32487, 2024; Economic Policy 2025)
URL: https://www.nber.org/papers/w32487

VERBATIM (abstract):
- "no more than a 0.66% increase in total factor productivity (TFP) over 10 years"
- "these macroeconomic effects appear nontrivial but modest"
- "predicted TFP gains over the next 10 years are even more modest and are predicted to be less than 0.53%"

Method (from paper/summaries): Hulten's theorem. AI exposed to ~20% of tasks, ~23% of those profitably automatable, ~27% avg task cost saving => modest aggregate effect. Warns early evidence is from easy-to-learn tasks; hard tasks may yield even less. Predicts AI reinforces inequality (capital + high-skill gain).
