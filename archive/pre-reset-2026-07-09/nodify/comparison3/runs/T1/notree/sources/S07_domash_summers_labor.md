# S07 — Domash & Summers, "How Tight Are U.S. Labor Markets?" (NBER w29739) and HKS/GrowthPolicy note on hard-landing risk (2022)

URLs:
- https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w29739/w29739.pdf
- https://www.hks.harvard.edu/centers/mrcbg/programs/growthpolicy/labor-market-view-risks-us-hard-landing-alex-domash-and

Verbatim (search-surfaced):
- "the vacancy and quit rates corresponded to a degree of labor market tightness previously associated with sub-2 percent unemployment rates."
- "The super-tight labor market coincided with wage inflation of 6.5 percent – the highest level experienced in the past 40 years – and firm-side slack measures predicted further increases in wage inflation."
- "Labor costs represent more than two-thirds of all business costs across the economy, meaning that wage inflation contributes significantly to underlying inflation and drastically increases the risk of a wage-price spiral."

Note: Domash-Summers (2022) forecast that disinflation would require a large rise in unemployment — a forecast that did NOT come true (see S11, S12, S14).
