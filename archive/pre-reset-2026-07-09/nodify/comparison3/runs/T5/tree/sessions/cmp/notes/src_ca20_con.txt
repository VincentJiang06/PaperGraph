Contrarian analysis of California AB 1228 ($20 fast-food minimum wage), as summarized by the Cato Institute research brief (based on NBER WP 34033 / related QCEW analyses).

Key findings (verbatim):
"Our median estimate suggests that California lost about 18,000 jobs that could have been retained if AB 1228 had not been passed."
"This approach, namely adjusting for the performance of other industries, suggests that AB 1228 reduced fast-food employment by 3.6 percent."
"After accounting for this trend, our findings suggest a greater employment decline of 3.2 percent due to AB 1228."

Note: Using QCEW fast-food series adjusted for other-industry/comparison trends, these analyses estimate a 3.2-3.6% employment decline (~18,000 jobs) from AB 1228 — directly contradicting the Berkeley null. The $20 floor is a high relative wage for fast food, so a negative estimate here is consistent with non-linearity at high Kaitz ratios. Evidence on AB 1228 employment is genuinely contested.
