# Jardim, Long, Plotnick, van Inwegen, Vigdor & Wething (2017/2022), "Minimum Wage Increases, Wages, and Low-Wage Employment: Evidence from Seattle", NBER WP 23532; + Evans School / UW summary
URLs:
- https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w23532/w23532.pdf
- https://evans.uw.edu/new-evidence-from-the-seattle-minimum-wage-study/

Verbatim quotes from NBER w23532 (pdftotext):
- "reduced hours worked in low-wage jobs by 6-7 percent, while hourly wages in such jobs increased by 3 percent. Consequently, total payroll for such jobs decreased, implying that the Ordinance lowered the amount paid to workers in low-wage jobs by an average of $74 per month per job in 2016."
- "hours worked by low-wage workers fell by about two hours per week, resulting in lower [earnings]" (from an embedded restatement)
- Effects concentrated: "the restaurant industry, and when examining total hours worked rather than employee headcount."

Verbatim quotes from Evans School "New Evidence" page (WebFetch):
- Effect on hourly wages: "wages rise by 3.4% once the city's minimum wage was $13"
- Effect on hours: "experiencing a 7.0% decrease in hours worked"
- Net effect: "net increase of around $12 per week" (for experienced workers, in the later/updated analysis)
- Hiring margin: "The team found evidence of a decline in the rate of hiring of low-wage workers who were not previously employed."

Note: The Seattle study is the leading US "negative" natural experiment (large disemployment on the HOURS margin from an $11->$13 step). Its interpretation is contested (EPI critique; the team's own later work found experienced incumbents roughly broke even/gained while the loss fell on reduced hiring of new low-wage entrants).
