# Congressional Budget Office (July 2019), "The Effects on Employment and Family Income of Increasing the Federal Minimum Wage"
Primary URL (PDF, returns 403 to fetch tools): https://www.cbo.gov/publication/55410
Verbatim restatement retrieved from Rice Kinder Institute Urban Edge (quoting CBO): https://kinder.rice.edu/urbanedge/15-minimum-wage-could-lift-13-million-out-poverty-and-potentially-cost-13-million-jobs

Verbatim quotes (from the accessible page, quoting CBO's July 2019 report):

- "The 1.3 million Americans that could lose their jobs if the minimum wage was increased to $15 is a median estimate, according to the report. The CBO economists wrote that resulting job losses could range between 'about zero and 3.7 million.'"

- "Raising the federal minimum wage to $15 an hour by 2025 would increase the pay of at least 17 million people."

- "1.3 million Americans would be lifted out of poverty" through the $15 minimum wage increase.

Interpretation: A neutral scorekeeper's CALIBRATED national projection for a large ($7.25 -> $15, roughly doubling) federal hike. The point estimate is NEGATIVE but modest relative to 17M raised; the confidence range straddles zero, reflecting exactly the Card-Krueger vs. competitive-model tension. Note this is a national uniform hike (very high bite in low-wage states), unlike the state/city natural experiments — so its more-negative tilt is consistent with the nonlinearity/relative-bite story.
