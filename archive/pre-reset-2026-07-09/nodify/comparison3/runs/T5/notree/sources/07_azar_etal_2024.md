# Azar, Huet-Vaughn, Marinescu, Taska & von Wachter (2024), "Minimum Wage Employment Effects and Labour Market Concentration", Review of Economic Studies 91(4); NBER WP 26101
URL: https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w26101/w26101.pdf

Verbatim quotes (from pdftotext of the NBER PDF):

- "more concentrated labor markets – where wages are more likely to be below marginal productivity – experience significantly more positive employment effects from the minimum [wage]."

- "workers in low concentration markets, minimum wage-induced employment changes become less negative as labor concentration increases, and are even estimated to be positive in the most highly concentrated markets. Our findings provide direct empirical evidence supporting the monopsony [model]."

- "A common explanation for the many null findings is the existence of substantial labor market monopsony or oligopsony (Manning, 2011; Naidu, Posner and Weyl, 2018). Under perfect [competition the minimum wage reduces employment; departing] from the assumption of a perfectly competitive labor market, the minimum wage can increase [employment] in the case of labor market monopsony."

Interpretation: this is the cleanest US natural-experiment test of the monopsony reconciliation. Employment effect sign FLIPS with labor-market concentration: negative in competitive (low-HHI) markets, ~zero to positive in concentrated (high-HHI) ones. The measured US "near-zero average" is a mix across market structures.
