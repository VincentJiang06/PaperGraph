# S10 — China's investment efficiency / ICOR (capital-return decline)
URLs: https://www.rieti.go.jp/en/china/04061801.html ("Why China's Investment Efficiency is Low")
      + World Robotics/ICOR literature (search-summarized)

Search-summarized findings:
- "China's ICOR has been on an upward trend since the 1990s, indicating a decline in investment efficiency.
  Since before the financial crisis, the ICOR has increased very sharply, indicating a marked deterioration
  in the efficiency of China's investment spending."
- "China's average ICOR for the years 1991 to 2003 was 4.1 ... Japan's ICOR during 1961-1970 was 3.2 ...
  Japan achieved 10.2% growth during its high-growth period."
- "The higher the ICOR, the lower the productivity of capital ... the return on investment is deteriorating
  over time."

Line relevance: L3/L6 ADVERSARIAL — the capital/automation offset faces DIMINISHING RETURNS: China already
needs more investment per unit of growth than Japan did, so "just add robots/capital" cannot fully substitute
for labor without productivity (TFP) doing the heavy lifting. Capital returns are FALLING (a China-worse point).
