Verbatim (RIETI):
- "the age structure of the population in China in 2020 was similar to that of Japan around 1990."
- "China has to face this severe challenge before it becomes affluent."
- "China's birthrate has been declining at a faster pace than that of Japan."

Search-summarized (speed/scale):
- "it took Japan 25 years for its population aged 65+ to grow from 10% to 30%, China is projected to make the
  same demographic shift in just 20 years."
- "China's absolute elderly population will be about four times larger than Japan's was at a similar stage.
  280 million people (19.8%) are aged 60+; on track to hit 400 million (28%) by 2035."
- "The IMF estimates it could reduce annual GDP growth by about 0.5 percentage points in the 2030s."

Search-summarized (development stage / "old before rich"):
- "China's GDP per capita reached $12,681 in 2023 ... Japan is positioned with a high elder share of around 36%
  and GDP per capita near $45,000."
- "China's median age in 2023 was the same as Japan's in 1995 ... share of people aged 65 and older matched
  Japan's in 1996." "Over the 12 years following these demographic milestones, Japan's average annual growth
  rate was 0.8% ... based on these historical trends, China's growth rate is likely to slow to 3% by 2028."

Line relevance: L1 (speed/scale WORSE than Japan), L2 (development stage: old-before-rich → convergence room is
the KEY China advantage; per-capita income at inflection far below Japan's).
