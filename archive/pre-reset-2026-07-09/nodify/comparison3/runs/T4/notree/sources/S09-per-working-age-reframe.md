# S09 — "Japan's lost decades" reframe: per-working-age-adult growth

Search-summarized finding (primary PDF returned as binary; number attributed to the paper):
- "From 1998 to 2019, Japan grew slightly faster than the U.S. in terms of GDP per working-age adult,
  with an accumulated growth of 31.9% compared to the U.S.'s 29.5%."
- "the working age population had already started to fall around 1995"; adjusting for it, "Japan records
  the highest growth rate among advanced economies in the 2000s" on output per working-age person.

Line relevance: L1/L7 KEY REFRAME — much of Japan's "stagnation" is an aggregate DENOMINATOR effect of a
shrinking workforce, not collapsing per-worker welfare. Cuts both ways: (a) "lost decades" overstate the
disaster; (b) but the AGGREGATE potential-growth drag from labor is real and mechanical.
