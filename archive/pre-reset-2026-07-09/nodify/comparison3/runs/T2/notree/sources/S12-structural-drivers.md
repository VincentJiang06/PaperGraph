# S12 — Structural drivers of inequality: skill-biased technological change (SBTC) & globalization (CEPR/VoxEU "Monetary policy and inequality: a new channel"; IMF WP/08/185 "Rising Income Inequality: Technology, or Trade and Financial Globalization?"; IMF SDN/15/13)
URLs: https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/monetary-policy-and-inequality-new-channel ; https://www.imf.org/external/pubs/ft/wp/2008/wp08185.pdf ; https://www.imf.org/external/pubs/ft/sdn/2015/sdn1513.pdf
(Findings surfaced via WebSearch; SDN/15/13 PDF 403'd.)

Near-verbatim (surfaced by WebSearch):
- "The usually discussed drivers of long-term trends in inequality have been skill-biased technological change, globalisation, and, more recently, automation and artificial intelligence."
- "Skill biased technological change represents a shift in production technology that favours more skilled and/or educated people and increases their relative productivity and wages, thus leading to higher inequality."
- "globalization, measured as financial and trade openness, has a moderate effect on inequality in comparison to technological progress." (IMF WP/08/185)

Relevance: These are the dominant STRUCTURAL explanations for the rise in INCOME inequality (and, feeding through savings, wealth inequality) since ~1980. They largely explain the trend but are slow-moving; they do not obviously generate the specific asset-valuation surge of 2009-2021. They mostly operate on the LABOR-income distribution, whereas QE's disequalizing channel is the ASSET/capital-gains distribution — partially distinct mechanisms.
