# S07 — Saez & Zucman, "The Rise of Income and Wealth Inequality in America" (JEP 2020, 34(4):3-26) and "Wealth Inequality in the US since 1913" (NBER w20625, 2016)
URLs: https://gabriel-zucman.eu/files/SaezZucman2020JEP.pdf ; https://www.nber.org/papers/w20625
(JEP PDF read directly, pages 1-2 confirm authorship/method. Specific top-share magnitudes surfaced verbatim via WebSearch of the w20625 abstract/text.)

Verbatim (from w20625 abstract / Saez-Zucman, surfaced by WebSearch):
- "The rise of wealth inequality is almost entirely due to the rise of the top 0.1% wealth share, from 7% in 1979 to 22% in 2012—a level almost as high as in 1929."
- "after a period of remarkable stability in the 1950s and 1960s, the top 0.1% wealth share reached its low-water mark in the 1970s, and since the early 1980s it has been gradually rising."
- "Between 1978 and 2018, the share of pre-tax income earned by the top 1 percent rose from 10 percent to about 19 percent, and the share of wealth owned by the top 0.1 percent rose from 7 percent to about 18 percent."

JEP 2020 verbatim (read directly, framing the long-run trend question):
- "This effort is motivated by the large and growing gap between the income recorded in the datasets traditionally used to study inequality... Who has benefited from economic growth since the 1980s?"

Relevance: US wealth concentration has risen on a steady structural trajectory since ~1980 — roughly three decades BEFORE QE began (2008) — establishing a large pre-existing/structural trend the QE-era increase sits on top of.
