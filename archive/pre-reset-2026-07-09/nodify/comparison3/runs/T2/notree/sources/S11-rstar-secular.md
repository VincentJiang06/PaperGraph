# S11 — Secular decline in the natural rate of interest r* (FRBSF EL 2017-27 "Demographic Transition and Low U.S. Interest Rates"; IMF WP 2022/030 "Secular Drivers of the Natural Rate of Interest in the US")
URLs: https://www.frbsf.org/wp-content/uploads/el2017-27.pdf ; https://www.elibrary.imf.org/view/journals/001/2022/030/article-A001-en.xml
(Findings surfaced via WebSearch.)

Near-verbatim (surfaced by WebSearch):
- "From the 1970s to the 2010s, r-star in the U.S. averaged around 2.5%, but by 2019, it had fallen to near-zero levels, a decline attributed to aging populations, slower productivity growth, and a global surplus of savings."
- "Rising income inequality, demographic change, and slowdown in productivity growth together explain most of the decline in the natural rate."
- On QE and stock prices: "QE had a significant but not exclusive effect on stock prices (DJIA, S&P500 and NASDAQ), suggesting that these stock prices are also affected by other missing variables and cyclical movements."

Relevance (RED-TEAM of the asset-price channel + identification): The equilibrium real rate fell for STRUCTURAL reasons (aging, savings glut, low productivity, and inequality ITSELF raises saving). Lower r* mechanically lifts asset valuations (lower discount rates) INDEPENDENT of QE — and QE is partly an endogenous RESPONSE to the same low-r* world (economy stuck at the effective lower bound). This makes QE and the structural forces collinear, and asset-price appreciation hard to attribute cleanly to QE vs secular decline in r*.
