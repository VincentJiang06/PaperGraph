# S10 — Population aging & wealth inequality (St. Louis Fed "The Effect of Aging on Wealth Inequality", 2017; Springer, Economic Change and Restructuring, "Population aging and wealth inequality", 2023)
URLs: https://www.stlouisfed.org/on-the-economy/2017/march/effect-aging-wealth-inequality ; https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10644-023-09551-3
(Findings surfaced via WebSearch.)

Verbatim / near-verbatim (surfaced by WebSearch):
- "Those under 35 years old represented over 25 percent of the population, but only about 5 percent of the wealth, while those aged 55 to 64 represented only about 16 percent of the population, but almost 31 percent of the wealth."
- Life-cycle effect INCREASES inequality: "wealth accumulation rises with age until retirement, with the wealthy continuing to save at high rates ... wealth inequality widens across the life course."
- Demographic effect REDUCES inequality: "a smaller share of young individuals with few assets makes the overall distribution more equal."
- "Top wealth shares follow a U path (decrease and then increase) in the process of population aging."

Relevance: Aging's effect on WEALTH inequality is two-directional (life-cycle vs demographic), i.e., ambiguous and gradual — it does not straightforwardly deliver a large QE-era jump in top wealth shares. Weakens the "aging would have done it anyway" counterfactual as a full substitute for the asset channel.
