S20 — Zanna Iscenko & Fabien Curto Millet (Economic Innovation Group), "Looking for the Ladder: Is AI
Impacting Entry-Level Jobs?" (Jan 2026)
URL: https://eig.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/TAWP-Iscenko-Millet.pdf
Facet/Line: L4 (youth/entry-level), L6 (ADVERSARIAL) — DIRECT CRITIQUE of S6 (Brynjolfsson "Canaries")
Tier: C (re-analysis of aggregate job-postings [Lightcast] + employment data; timing/confound argument)

DESIGN: Not a new causal estimate — a REBUTTAL. Re-examines the timing of the entry-level (22–25)
employment decline that S6 attributes to AI; tests it against (a) the AI-adoption timeline and
(b) the 2022 monetary-tightening / interest-rate-sensitivity confound.
EFFECT + DIRECTION: DISPUTES the AI-attribution of S6. Argues the youth decline is mostly the lagged
consequence of a hiring freeze that PREDATES ChatGPT, plus a mechanical "aging illusion."

VERBATIM QUOTES:
- "The central weakness of the hypothesis that AI is the main driver of the recent entry-level downturn is
  its implausible timeline."
- "The downturn in labor demand for AI-exposed occupations — using the same definition of exposure used by
  the 'Canaries' paper — began long before ChatGPT's launch ... vacancies for the highest AI exposure
  quintile of occupations peaked in March–April 2022 and declined sharply throughout the remainder of the
  year. This hiring slowdown predates any plausible generative AI effect by over six months."
- "'AI exposure' and 'interest rate sensitivity' are deeply correlated variables ... occupations in the top
  quintile of AI exposure are overwhelmingly concentrated in sectors like 'Information', 'Finance and
  Insurance', and 'Professional and Technical Services'." (The Fed began its most aggressive hiking cycle
  in forty years in March 2022 — "precisely when job postings in these sectors began to fall.")
- Aging-illusion: a full hiring freeze with ZERO layoffs makes the 22–25 cohort "shrunk by a shocking 25
  percent while it remained level for all other age groups" — "aging can create the statistical illusion of
  a targeted decline in the youngest cohort."
- "There is no evidence that job postings for junior roles within occupations most exposed to AI have
  declined more than postings for senior positions."

LIMITS: EIG is a policy think-tank (not peer-reviewed); an interpretive/timing rebuttal, not a fresh causal
estimate. Concedes real distress: "LinkedIn July 2025 data, entry-level hiring rates have declined 23
percent compared to pre-pandemic levels, a steeper drop than the 18 percent decline for overall hiring,"
and explicitly does NOT rule out FUTURE AI displacement. RESOLVES GAP 1: a credible confound (monetary
policy + cohort mechanics) rivals AI as the driver of the S6 pattern.
