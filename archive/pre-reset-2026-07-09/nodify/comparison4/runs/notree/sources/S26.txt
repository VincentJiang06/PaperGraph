S26 — Federal Reserve Board (FEDS Notes) — "AI Adoption and Firms' Job-Posting Behavior"
March 27, 2026. URL: https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/
ai-adoption-and-firms-job-posting-behavior-20260327.html
Tier C — firm/industry-level observational (job postings vs AI adoption). Lines: L2, L6.
(Supporting: US Census BTOS 2026 firm survey, Tier D.)

DESIGN/SAMPLE: Links firm- and industry-level AI adoption/investment measures to subsequent
job-posting (Lightcast/vacancy) behavior; regression models across firms and industries.

VERBATIM QUOTES (Fed note):
- "there is no evidence of a reduction in job postings for industries or firms which have
   higher levels of AI adoption."
- coefficients on industry AI adoption "are generally positive, indicating that there is no
   evidence thus far that industries with higher levels of AI adoption are posting fewer jobs."
- "There is no evidence across the range of models that firm-level AI investment is having a
   negative impact on subsequent job-posting behavior."
- "we find no evidence of negative impacts thus far on firms' job-posting behavior"
- CAVEAT (authors): results examine "the total level of job postings in firms and industries,
   rather than on specific occupations which may be particularly susceptible to automation via
   AI" — i.e. aggregate stability can mask specific-occupation pressure.

SUPPORTING (US Census, firm survey / BTOS, Tier D):
- Census summary: "in most cases, adoption of new technologies like robotics and Artificial
   Intelligence had no impact on worker numbers or skill level."
- BTOS Nov 2025–Jan 2026: ~18% of firms use AI (32% employment-weighted); most users rely on
   AI to AUGMENT tasks; AI-related employment DECREASES are rare (~2% of firms). [secondhand]

EFFECT + DIRECTION: NULL at the firm/industry level — AI-adopting firms are NOT posting
fewer jobs (coefficients if anything positive). Strengthens L6 (aggregate/average skeptic
line) at a NEW level of aggregation (the firm), complementing admin-worker nulls S5/S13/S21.
LIMIT: total postings, not occupation-specific; adoption endogenous (fast-growing firms adopt);
short horizon; Census augment/2% figures are self-report survey (Tier D).
