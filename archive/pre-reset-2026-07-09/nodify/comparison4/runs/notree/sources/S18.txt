S18 — Philippe Aghion & Simon Bunel, "AI and Growth: Where Do We Stand?" (FRBSF, June 2024)
URL: https://www.frbsf.org/wp-content/uploads/AI-and-Growth-Aghion-Bunel.pdf
Facet/Line: L2, L3, L6 (macro ceiling) — COUNTER-VIEW to Acemoglu S14
Tier: D / structural model (task-based Acemoglu-2024 formula + historical-analogy approach; forecast,
not first-hand outcome data). Included for BALANCE against S14.

DESIGN: Two approaches. (1) Historical analogy to electricity/digital tech waves. (2) The SAME task-based
model Acemoglu (2024) uses, but with Aghion-Bunel's own reading of the empirical literature for each
component (AI-exposed task share, profitability of AI use, cost savings, labor share).
EFFECT + DIRECTION: MUCH LARGER macro upside than Acemoglu — median ~0.68pp/yr extra TFP (vs Acemoglu 0.07pp).

VERBATIM QUOTES:
- "Based on the first approach, we estimate that the AI revolution should increase aggregate productivity
  growth by between 0.8 and 1.3pp per year over the next decade."
- "Using the second approach but with our own reading of the recent empirical literature on the various
  components of the task-based formula, we obtain a median estimate of 0.68pp additional annual total
  factor productivity (TFP) growth."
- "As for the second approach ... it leads Acemoglu (2024) to a much smaller extra growth estimate of
  0.07 pp per year over the next decade ... we estimate that the AI revolution should increase aggregate
  productivity growth by between 0.07pp and 1.24pp, with a median estimate of 0.68pp."
- "This estimate in turn may be seen as a lower bound."
- On the divergence: they retain some Acemoglu values (labor share 0.57) but use HIGHER AI-exposure and
  cost-saving inputs: "The share of tasks exposed to AI is [0.185; 0.68]" and profitable-to-use "[0.23; 0.8]."

LIMITS: Same Tier-D critique as S14 — a forecast from a stylized model, sensitive to input assumptions;
gap with Acemoglu (0.07 vs 0.68, ~10x) is driven by contested exposure/cost-saving inputs, NOT new data.
Confirms the macro question is UNSETTLED within one framework. Neither is first-hand outcome evidence.
