VERBATIM POINTS (report, via Fortune coverage + Budget Lab):
- "The picture of AI's impact on the labor market that emerges from our data is one that largely reflects stability, not major disruption at an economy-wide level."
- "While anxiety over the effects of AI on today's labor market is widespread, our data suggests it remains largely speculative."
- Gimbel: "No matter which way you look at the data, at this exact moment, it just doesn't seem like there's major macroeconomic effects here."
- Method/finding (Budget Lab summary): occupational dissimilarity, industry dissimilarity, and exposure/usage metrics "remain flat or within historical ranges or prior trends"; "measures of AI exposure, automation, and augmentation show no sign of being related to changes in employment or unemployment to date." "no discernible disruption since ChatGPT's release 33 months ago."
- Nuance: "While the occupational mix is changing faster than it has in the past, it is not a large difference and predates the widespread introduction of AI."
- Caveat: "technological change does not happen in a vacuum" — a recession could accelerate impacts; explicitly "not predictive of the future."
