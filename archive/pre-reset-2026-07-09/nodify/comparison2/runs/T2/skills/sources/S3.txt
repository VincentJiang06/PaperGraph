Title: What Caused the US Pandemic-Era Inflation? (NBER Working Paper 31417 / PIIE Working Paper)
Authors: Ben Bernanke, Olivier Blanchard
Publisher: NBER / Peterson Institute for International Economics (PIIE)
URL (PIIE): https://www.piie.com/publications/working-papers/2023/what-caused-us-pandemic-era-inflation
URL (NBER): https://www.nber.org/papers/w31417
Date: 2023 (published in AEA Journal of Macroeconomics 2025, doi 10.1257/mac.20230195)

Key verbatim extracted content:

Abstract summary: The paper analyzes "the direct and indirect effects of product-market and labor-market shocks on prices and nominal wages" to quantify inflation sources during the pandemic era.

Main finding: "most of the inflation surge that began in 2021 was the result of shocks to prices given wages" rather than labor market overheating.

Specific shock sources: "These shocks included sharp increases in commodity prices, reflecting strong aggregate demand, and sectoral price spikes, resulting from changes in the level and sectoral composition of demand together with constraints on sectoral supply."

Labor market's secondary but persistent role: "Although tight labor markets have thus far not been the primary driver of inflation, the authors find that the effects of overheated labor markets on nominal wage growth and inflation are more persistent than the effects of product-market shocks."

Model structure: builds on a wage Phillips curve, a mark-up price-setting equation, and adaptive equations for short- and long-run inflation expectations; drivers include the vacancies-to-unemployed ratio, energy and food price shocks, a measure of supply-chain disruptions (shortages), Covid lockdowns/reopenings.
