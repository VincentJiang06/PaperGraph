Extracted content:

Adam Shapiro proposes "a framework to decompose inflation into supply- and demand-driven components," generating two new data series that quantify the degree to which either demand or supply is driving PCE inflation in a given month.

Methodology quote: "Demand-driven categories are identified as those where an unexpected change in price moves in the same direction as the change in quantity in a given month. Supply-driven categories are identified as those where unexpected changes in price and quantity move in opposite directions."

Findings: "The demand-driven contribution tends to decline during recessions, while the supply-driven contribution tends to follow food and energy prices... oil-supply shocks act to increase the supply driven contribution, but decrease the demand-driven contribution of inflation."

2023 context (from search summary of data tool): "a substantial portion of core PCE inflation has been driven by demand rather than supply. As supply chains have recovered, supply-driven inflation has receded but remains well above pre-pandemic levels. In contrast, demand-driven inflation has continued to grow in recent quarters, despite tighter monetary policy."

Data tool page notes: "Figure 1 shows the contributions to annualized monthly changes in PCE inflation for headline PCE inflation, and Figure 2 shows the same for core PCE inflation." Bars represent supply-driven (green), demand-driven (blue), and ambiguous categories.
