Congress.gov CRS Insight IN12410, "The Sahm Rule Trigger: Is the United States in a Recession?"; plus Fortune reporting ("Recession indicator: Sahm Rule flashes red, but creator says 'this time really could be different'," Aug 2024; "Sneaking unemployment rate means the U.S. economy is inching closer to triggering Sahm Rule," Dec 2025).

In July 2024, the Sahm rule was triggered when the three-month moving average of the unemployment rate was 0.53 percentage points higher than its low since July 2023. Sahm herself indicated that despite her rule triggering, she did not believe the United States was in a recession at that time, though she believed recession risk had increased -- attributing part of the unemployment-rate rise to labor-supply growth (e.g., higher immigration-driven labor force growth) rather than to collapsing labor demand, which is why "this time really could be different." The U.S. economy did not fall into recession in 2024, in part because the Fed engineered a "soft landing" via interest-rate cuts; NBER-tracked recession indicators were not consistent with a recession, with real GDP growing 1.4% and 2.8% in Q1 and Q2 2024 respectively. By late 2025 the situation remained precarious: unemployment rose to 4.3% by August 2025 (up 0.6 percentage points from the cycle low of 3.7% in January 2024), and if unemployment reached 4.6% in December 2025 the Sahm Rule would trigger again.
