San Francisco Fed research series on pandemic-era excess savings: "The Rise and Fall of Pandemic Excess Savings" (Economic Letter 2023-15, May 2023); "Data Revisions and Pandemic-Era Excess Savings" (blog, Nov 2023); "Pandemic Savings Are Gone: What's Next for U.S. Consumers?" (blog, May 2024).

Households rapidly accumulated unprecedented levels of excess savings that amounted to about $2.1 trillion during the pandemic. Initial estimates suggested pandemic-era excess savings would be fully depleted during the third quarter of 2023. Following comprehensive data revisions, the amount of pandemic-era excess savings still available was estimated to be larger than previously expected and likely to last into the first half of 2024: households held about $430 billion of aggregate excess savings by September 2023. As of March 2024, the San Francisco Fed's estimates showed that overall excess savings in the U.S. economy had been fully depleted (a finding subject to some debate, with alternative methodologies suggesting a remaining stock of at least $1.6 trillion as of May 2024, particularly considering household heterogeneity by income level).
