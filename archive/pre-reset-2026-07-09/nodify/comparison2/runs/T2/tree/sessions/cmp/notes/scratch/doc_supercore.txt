FRBSF Economic Letter 2023-19, "Will a Cooler Labor Market Slow Supercore Inflation?" (San Francisco Fed, July 2023); plus general reporting on "supercore" (core services excluding shelter) inflation dynamics.

Supercore inflation -- core services CPI/PCE excluding rent of primary residence and owners' equivalent rent -- has remained steadily high since the middle of 2022. Supercore inflation has a much weaker relationship to the unemployment gap compared to rent inflation, with an estimated slope of -0.09 that is not statistically significant, suggesting that a slowdown in economic activity may lead to only a muted decline in supercore inflation -- highlighting its stickiness. Supercore is the most labor-intensive component of core CPI and especially sensitive to nominal wage increases; although nominal wage increases moderated during 2023, they remained above pre-pandemic levels, continuing to put upward pressure on supercore inflation. Meanwhile supply chains, as measured by the GSCPI, have been improving steadily since the start of 2022, helping cool core goods inflation, which follows the GSCPI with a lag of several months.
