Federal Reserve rate-decision reporting: FOMC statements and press coverage (Federal Reserve Board press release, Dec 10, 2025; CNBC "Fed interest rate decision December 2025"; CBS News reporting on 2024-2025 rate path).

The Fed held the federal-funds target range at 5.25%-5.50% from July 2023 to September 2024 -- the peak of the tightening cycle. The Fed then reduced rates three times between September and December 2024 (including an initial 50-basis-point cut), a cumulative 1 percentage point of cuts, bringing the target range to 4.25%-4.50%. After holding rates unchanged for the first nine months of 2025, the FOMC resumed cutting, delivering a third consecutive cut in as many meetings by December 10, 2025, bringing the target range to 3.50%-3.75%. At the December 2025 meeting, one voting member (Stephen I. Miran) dissented in favor of a larger 50-basis-point cut. Market-based measures of the expected federal-funds path implied a further decline to about 3.3% by end-2026. Separately, core PCE inflation eased to 2.1% year-on-year in September 2024, then remained "somewhat elevated" through early 2025, receding from 2.9% in December 2024 to 2.5% in April 2025 -- still above the Fed's 2% target.
