Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell, selected speeches on inflation causes: "Restoring Price Stability" (March 21, 2022); "Monetary Policy and Price Stability" (Jackson Hole, August 26, 2022); "Inflation and the Labor Market" (Brookings, November 30, 2022); "Economic Outlook" (Jackson Hole, August 25, 2023).

March 2022: "the pandemic and the associated shutdown and reopening of the economy caused a serious upheaval in many parts of the economy, snarling supply chains, constraining labor supply, and creating a major boom in demand for goods." Also: "growth slows as the very fast growth from the early stages of reopening fades, the effects of fiscal support wane, and monetary policy accommodation is removed."

November 2022: "the inflation that we saw at the beginning of this episode in, back in March of 21, was not really related to wages at all. It was related to tightness in, in goods markets, largely due to supply chain issues."

August 2023: "the ongoing episode of high inflation initially emerged from a collision between very strong demand and pandemic-constrained supply. By the time the Federal Open Market Committee raised the policy rate in March 2022, it was clear that bringing down inflation would depend on both the unwinding of the unprecedented pandemic-related demand and supply distortions" [and tighter monetary policy].

August 2022 (Jackson Hole): Powell warned that restoring price stability "will bring some pain to households and businesses," signaling willingness to accept labor-market softening to bring down inflation regardless of its supply- vs demand-driven composition.
