Domash, Alex & Summers, Lawrence H. (2022), "How Tight Are U.S. Labor Markets?" NBER Working Paper w29739.

Since the start of the pandemic, demand-side labor market measures like the vacancy rate and the quits rate surged to record highs, deviating significantly from their historical relationship with supply-side measures like the unemployment rate. Vacancy and quit rates currently experienced in the United States correspond to a degree of labor market tightness previously associated with sub-2 percent unemployment rates. The firm-side predicted unemployment rate has essentially all the explanatory power in predicting wage inflation over the period 2001 to 2019; this represents the authors' preferred measure for assessing excess demand and inflationary pressure in the labor market. With inflation accelerating at its fastest rate in four decades and unemployment falling below 4%, the authors argue indicators pointed to an overheating U.S. economy. In February 2022, year-over-year wage inflation reached 6.5 percent according to the Federal Reserve Bank of Atlanta wage tracker, the highest on record.
