Storm, Servaas (2024), "Tilting at Windmills: Bernanke and Blanchard's Obsession with the Wage-Price Spiral," International Journal of Political Economy (also INET Working Paper No. 220).

Storm argues that the Bernanke-Blanchard model "sets monetary policymakers up to deliver significantly more monetary tightening than can be justified on the basis of more realistic model analyses." A key point in the critique is that U.S. real wages are not downwardly rigid but have instead been falling relative to labor productivity -- rather than being a source of inflationary pressure, real wages acted as an absorber of inflationary shocks. Storm argues Bernanke and Blanchard exaggerate the inflationary consequences of a tight labor market and of cost shocks by building real-wage rigidity into their model through the inflation-expectations channel, and that this shapes a policy-relevant narrative (wage-price spiral risk) that argues for more aggressive tightening than the underlying supply-shock-dominated decomposition actually supports.
