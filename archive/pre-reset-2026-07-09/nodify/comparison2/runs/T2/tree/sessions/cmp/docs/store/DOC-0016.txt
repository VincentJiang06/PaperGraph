Money-supply and energy-price background data, compiled from multiple sources: St. Louis Fed "The Rise and Fall of M2" (May 2023); Montclair State working paper "Money Supply and Inflation after COVID-19"; U.S. Congress Joint Economic Committee (Republican) analysis "To Combat Rising Energy Prices, Unleash American Production" (Feb 2022); BLS "Consumer Price Index: 2022 in review."

M2 money supply grew 19% from 2019 to 2020 and a further 16% in 2021 -- far above the roughly 6%/year average growth from 2000-2019. Headline PCE inflation peaked in June 2022, about 18 months after the peak of M2 growth, by which time M2 growth had already fallen back to historically unremarkable levels -- a lag pattern consistent with, but not dispositive of, a monetary-driven demand channel operating with a long transmission lag. On energy: in December 2021, CPI inflation reached 7.0% year-on-year (a 40-year high), of which roughly 21% (about 1.5 percentage points) was attributable to rising energy prices; gasoline prices rose 49.6% over 2021. From December 2021 to December 2022, energy prices rose a further 7.3%, about a quarter of the prior year's pace, while gasoline prices fell 1.5% in 2022 after the 49.6% rise in 2021 -- illustrating how a large share of the 2021 CPI spike, and a declining share by 2022, was attributable to volatile energy components.
