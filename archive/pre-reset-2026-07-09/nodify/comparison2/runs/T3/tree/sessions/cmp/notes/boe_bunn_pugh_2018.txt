Bunn, Philip; Pugh, Alice; Yeates, Chris. "The distributional impact of monetary policy easing in the UK between 2008 and 2014." Bank of England Staff Working Paper No. 720, March 2018.
Summarized via companion Bank Underground blog post (2018-03-27): https://bankunderground.co.uk/2018/03/27/how-does-monetary-policy-affect-the-distribution-of-income-and-wealth/
Primary paper: https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/working-paper/2018/the-distributional-impact-of-monetary-policy-easing-in-the-uk-between-2008-and-2014

VERBATIM QUOTES (from Bank Underground summary of the staff working paper):

On overall effect on inequality (Gini):
"We estimate that looser monetary policy only had a small impact on the income and wealth Gini coefficients between 2008 and 2014."
"monetary policy had very little impact on relative measures of inequality."

On age-group divergence (income vs wealth channels point in OPPOSITE directions across age groups):
"younger households are estimated to have benefited most from higher income in cash terms"
"older households gained more from higher wealth."
"households where the head was under the age of 40 saw a reduction in their real incomes between 2006-08 and 2012-14, while those aged over 65 saw a rise." [note: this reflects broader income trends, not solely attributable to MP, per the paper's own framing]

On wealth-decile distribution (counter to naive "MP loot the poor to enrich the rich" story):
"households in the bottom half of the wealth distribution are estimated to have experienced a slightly larger percentage increase in real wealth than those at the top."

On savers vs borrowers direction (confirms redistribution but the paper frames it as small/multi-directional in net effect):
"The cut in Bank Rate reduced household interest payments and receipts, redistributing from savers to borrowers."

Methodology note: study uses ONS Wealth and Assets Survey panel microdata for the UK, covering both the Bank Rate cuts and QE gilt purchases 2008-2014, and decomposes effects into income channel and wealth/asset-price channel by household age/income/wealth decile.

Relevance: This is the single most granular microdata-based distributional study of the exact 2008-2014 QE+low-rate period, and directly refutes a simple monotonic "QE redistributes from poor savers to rich asset holders" narrative on THREE counts: (a) aggregate Gini effect is small; (b) income effects favor the young while wealth effects favor the old (retirees), so which is "worse off" depends on which channel and age cohort one focuses on; (c) in percentage terms, bottom-half wealth holders gained slightly MORE than top wealth holders (though top holders gained far more in cash terms) -- i.e. direction is genuinely ambiguous/contested rather than uniformly regressive, consistent with our tree's N-0008 framing.
