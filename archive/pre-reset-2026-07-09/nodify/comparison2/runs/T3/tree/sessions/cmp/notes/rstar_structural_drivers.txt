Sources on structural (non-monetary-policy) drivers of the decline in the neutral real interest rate (r-star), independent of QE:

(A) Rachel, Lukasz; Summers, Lawrence H. "On Secular Stagnation in the Industrialized World." NBER Working Paper 26198, 2019. Summarized in NBER Digest (Nov 2019): https://www.nber.org/digest/nov19/secular-stagnation-and-decline-real-interest-rates
- "the neutral real global interest rate — the rate which balances desired saving and investment and thus leads to full employment and stable inflation — has fallen about 3 percentage points since the 1970s... the average neutral real rate is around zero in industrialized nations."
- "The industrial world's working-age population will decline over the next generation, and these demographic developments eliminate the demand for new capital goods to equip and house a growing workforce." (demographic/aging channel, structural and independent of central bank asset purchases)
- Rachel & Summers (2019) also find that absent rising government debt/deficits and social insurance expansion, the neutral rate would have fallen ~7 percentage points (vs the observed ~3pp), meaning demographics+productivity trends alone push r* down even further than observed -- i.e. these forces are large enough to explain a low/negative-rate world on their own, without invoking QE as a cause.

(B) Federal Reserve Bank of San Francisco, "Underlying Trends in the U.S. Neutral Interest Rate," Economic Letter, April 2025. https://www.frbsf.org/research-and-insights/publications/economic-letter/2025/04/underlying-trends-in-us-neutral-interest-rate/
- "U.S. population aging was an important factor pushing r-star down" in the pre-2019 period.
- "International factors, as captured by the global rate, and U.S. population aging contributed significantly to pushing U.S. real rates down" before 2019.
- "In both periods, the estimates suggest that TFP [productivity] has played a very minor role in driving r-star" (contradicts naive productivity-slowdown-only stories; aging/global factors dominate).
- Notably, this decomposition of r-star drivers is conducted entirely in terms of demographic, fiscal, and global-capital-flow variables; it does not treat Fed asset purchases/QE as a structural driver of r-star -- QE is conventionally modeled as a policy RESPONSE to a low r-star environment, not a determinant of the trend estimate itself.

Relevance: both sources independently corroborate that a substantial, quantifiable share of the secular decline in real interest rates (which is the proximate driver of the "reach for yield"/valuation effects on stocks, bonds and housing) is attributable to demographic aging and global saving/investment imbalances that operate on a multi-decade horizon predating and independent of the Fed's 2008-2021 QE programs. This underpins the argument that a substantial part of the "resulting" asset-price and wealth-share appreciation attributed to QE (cf. DOC-0001, DOC-0002) would plausibly have occurred, at least partially, in a QE-free counterfactual because the underlying r* was already being pushed down by these structural forces.
