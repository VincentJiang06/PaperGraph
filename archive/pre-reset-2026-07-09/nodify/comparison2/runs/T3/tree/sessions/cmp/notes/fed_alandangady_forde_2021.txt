Aladangady, Aditya; Forde, Akaisha. "Wealth Inequality and the Racial Wealth Gap." FEDS Notes, Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System, October 22, 2021.
URL: https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/wealth-inequality-and-the-racial-wealth-gap-accessible-20211022.htm

KEY EXTRACTED STATISTICS (US wealth Gini coefficient by year, from the note's data tables):
2007: 0.82
2010: 0.85
2013: 0.85
2016: 0.86
2019: 0.85

Relevance: shows that the bulk of the post-crisis rise in the US wealth Gini coefficient (roughly +0.03) occurred rapidly between 2007 and 2010 -- i.e., during and immediately after the financial crisis and QE1 -- and then PLATEAUED (0.85-0.86) through 2019, rather than climbing steadily through the entire 2008-2021 QE period. This total wealth-Gini increase (~0.03) is roughly 2-3x larger than the ~0.012-0.013 Gini contribution that Medlin & Epstein (DOC-0002) attribute to conventional expansionary monetary policy over a comparable multi-decade window, implying that non-monetary-policy factors (equity ownership concentration trends, income-inequality-driven differential saving as in Mian-Straub-Sufi (DOC-0003), demographic/composition effects) plausibly account for a majority of the observed wealth-Gini increase. This is relevant to the magnitude question in N-0011: even a real and well-documented employment/wage-compression channel (DOC-0008, offsetting ~1/3 of a 40-year WAGE inequality trend) operates on labor income, not directly on the wealth-Gini metric, which is dominated by capital/asset ownership concentration -- so the two channels are not mechanically comparable or netting against each other on the same metric.
