National Low Income Housing Coalition, summary of Center for Responsible Lending / United for a Fair Economy-type research on the 2007-2011 foreclosure crisis's racial/wealth disparities.
URL: https://nlihc.org/resource/report-shows-african-americans-lost-half-their-wealth-due-housing-crisis-and-unemployment

VERBATIM EXCERPTS:
"African Americans have lost over half of their wealth since the beginning of the recession through falling homeownership rates and loss of jobs."
"Since 2007, nearly 8% of African Americans and Latinos have lost their homes to foreclosure compared to 4.5% of non-Hispanic whites at similar income levels."
"African Americans are more than 70% more likely to have been foreclosed upon than non-Hispanic whites."

Relevance: documents the severity and regressivity of the pre-QE/early-QE-era financial-crisis recession ITSELF (foreclosure + job loss), independent of and prior to sustained QE support -- illustrating what a deeper, longer, unmitigated recession (the relevant counterfactual against which QE's net distributional effect should be judged) does to low-wealth and minority households: wealth losses on the order of 50%, several times the foreclosure rate of white households. This is the empirical backdrop for the N-0009 counterfactual argument that averting a deeper/longer downturn (via QE-supported demand stabilization, cf. DOC-0007) plausibly protected low-wealth households from an even larger version of this same damage, rather than only "helping the rich" via asset prices.
