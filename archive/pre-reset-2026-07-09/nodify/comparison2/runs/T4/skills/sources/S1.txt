Title: Is ageing China facing a Japanese-style "lost decade"?
Source: Merics (Mercator Institute for China Studies)
URL: https://merics.org/en/comment/ageing-china-facing-japanese-style-lost-decade

Key verbatim/paraphrased extraction (via fetch):
- Opening thesis: "With appropriately targeted polices, China will avoid a 'lost decade.'"
- Development stage: China is aging "before it is rich," retaining convergence growth potential with 6% annual GDP growth, whereas Japan was already an advanced economy when aging intensified with established slower growth rates.
- Consumer behavior: the older Chinese generation "have never...been important drivers of consumption," contrasting with Japan where the younger cohort "feels less economically prosperous than the older population segment."
- Human capital: Unlike Japan where "the education gap between generations is narrow," China's human capital is "dramatically skewed in favor of the young," enabling productivity maintenance as workers decline.
- Dependency ratios: "China's dependency ratio...will remain lower than Japan's for some years to come."
- Caveat: pension sustainability remains a major concern requiring ongoing government intervention and asset transfers to cover liabilities.
