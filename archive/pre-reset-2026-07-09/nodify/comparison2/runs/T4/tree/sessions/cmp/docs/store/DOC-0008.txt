Key figures:
- China's GDP per capita (PPP) in 2024: approximately $27,105 (Trading Economics/World Bank data), vs. US GDP per capita (PPP) of approximately $85,810 in 2024 -- i.e., China is at roughly 29%-32% of the US PPP per-capita level as of 2024. (Econbrowser commenter citing World Bank data independently put China per-capita income at "$25,000 while the US's is over $85,300" -- roughly 29%, consistent with the above.)
- Japan's GDP per capita (PPP) in 1990 was approximately $35,272 against a US level of roughly $43,742 (approx. 1991) -- i.e. Japan was at roughly 80% of the US PPP per-capita level heading into its bubble collapse. Separately, Japan's (nominal, market-exchange-rate) GDP grew from about 9% of the US level in 1960 to about 73% by 1995 in some estimates; due to strong yen appreciation after the Plaza Accord, Japan's NOMINAL (non-PPP) GDP per capita briefly exceeded the US level around 1995 (estimated at roughly 1.4x the US nominal level that year), even as PPP-adjusted (cost-of-living-adjusted) per-capita income remained below the US.
- By 2024, Japan's total GDP had fallen to about 14% of the total US GDP level (reflecting three decades of yen depreciation and stagnant growth after the "Lost Decades" began), illustrating how far a "mature, already-caught-up" economy can still fall in relative terms after stagnation sets in.

Interpretation: Regardless of which measure (PPP vs. nominal) is used, Japan entered its stagnation already close to the US income frontier (roughly 73-140% of the US level depending on measure), i.e., it had largely exhausted its "catch-up" growth potential before 1990. China in 2024 remains at only about 29-32% of the US PPP per-capita level, i.e., far below the income frontier, implying China retains substantially more theoretical catch-up/convergence growth potential than Japan had at the onset of its lost decades -- a structural difference in initial conditions.
