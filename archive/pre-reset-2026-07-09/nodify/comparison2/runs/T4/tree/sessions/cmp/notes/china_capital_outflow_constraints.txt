URLs: https://www.scmp.com/economy/china-economy/article/3304476/china-may-face-sustained-capital-outflow-pressure-top-economists-warn ; https://www.centralbanking.com/central-banks/currency/7860946/chinas-capital-controls-here-to-stay

Key points:
- China's capital account posted a deficit of US$496.2 billion in 2024, described as a historic high; outflows attributed to uncertainty over US Federal Reserve policy, US tariff actions, geopolitical tensions, and weak domestic consumer sentiment.
- Net foreign direct investment (FDI) into China plunged from peak inflows of $334 billion in 2021 to net OUTFLOWS of nearly $154 billion in 2024 -- a roughly $488 billion swing over three years.
- Structural constraint (Impossible Trinity): an open economy like China's cannot simultaneously have free cross-border capital flows, an independent monetary policy, and a stable nominal exchange rate. China's official policy still nominally favors capital account liberalization, but in practice liberalization has slowed: capital INFLOWS continue to be encouraged while capital OUTFLOWS are increasingly restricted, specifically to ease depreciation pressure on the yuan.
- Implication for policy space: even though China's policy interest rate has not hit the zero lower bound (unlike Japan for most of its "lost decades"), the government's ability to cut rates aggressively is constrained by the risk of accelerating capital outflows and yuan depreciation -- i.e., China's monetary policy space is not simply "more room to cut" in isolation; it is bounded by the capital-account/exchange-rate side of the Impossible Trinity, a constraint Japan (with an already fully open capital account and, at the time, a structurally strong/appreciating yen) did not face in the same form during its own lost decades.
