## GDP and Income Levels
The report notes that "China now is a poorer country than Japan was then, with still more convergence opportunities ahead." This suggests China has greater potential for catch-up growth, unlike Japan which was already developed when its bubble burst.

## Capital Returns and Investment
The document identifies a critical similarity: "over-investment that preceded Japan's lost decade and led to a low rate of return on capital." China faces comparable dynamics, with both nations pursuing manufacturing expansion despite diminishing returns.

## Debt Accumulation
A striking parallel emerges: "Japan has accumulated public debt very rapidly since the bursting of the bubble, reaching levels above 230 percent of GDP today. China is following the same path of accumulation of fiscal deficits." Both nations struggle with debt management post-bubble.

## Savings-Investment Imbalances
The authors emphasize that "China has barely reduced its imbalances, characterised by high excess savings and low private consumption," distinguishing it from Japan's eventual consumption-driven transition.

## Policy Response Similarities
Both nations adopted hesitant monetary policies: "the monetary policy of the People's Bank of China...was also too tight, at least when compared to the rest of the world," mirroring Japan's initial response.

## Key Uncertainty
The authors conclude: "It remains unclear – though globally significant – whether China will be able to deviate from the path followed by Japan in the 1980s and 1990s."
