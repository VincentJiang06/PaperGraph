## Similarities
Koo identifies a fundamental parallel: both nations experienced real estate bubbles that burst, leading populations to realize assets were overpriced. He notes that "once the real estate bubble burst and people realized that they were chasing what I call wrong asset prices, unsustainable asset prices, which was similar in Japan 34 years ago," citizens stopped borrowing and began debt repayment.

## Key Differences — China's Informational Advantage
The most crucial distinction involves awareness. Koo emphasizes: "Everyone in China knows about the balance sheet recession. That's a huge advantage China has over Japan 34 years ago." This knowledge enables faster governmental intervention before economic damage accumulates.

## Structural Complications
However, China faces complications Japan didn't. While "The Japanese bubble was really just a bubble," China's construction sector comprises 26 percent of GDP. Koo warns that "construction is down very sharply, which means that industry is shrinking very rapidly," creating a dual crisis combining balance sheet problems with direct industrial collapse.
