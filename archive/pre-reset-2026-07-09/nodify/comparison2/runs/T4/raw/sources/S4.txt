## Total Debt Levels
China's total social financing reached "RMB 430.2 trillion, up 8.9% year-on-year" by mid-2025, while nominal GDP grew only 4.1%. This created a significant imbalance: the total social financing-to-GDP ratio climbed "from 303% of GDP at the end of 2024 to 309% six months later."

## Global Context
The IMF data cited indicates that "debt has grown so rapidly in China that the nation accounts for more than half of the increase in the global economy's debt-to-GDP ratio since 2008." Among major economies, only Japan has a higher debt-to-GDP ratio, though this makes China particularly notable as a middle-income economy.

## Japan Comparison
Japan entered its adjustment phase in the early 1990s with "government debt was roughly 40% of GDP," according to World Bank data. Despite this initial advantage, Japan experienced a difficult transition where "its share of global GDP dropped from 17-18% at its peak to 9-10% two decades later."

## Policy Space Implications
The author emphasizes that "China still has policy space to act, mainly in the form of a strong central-government balance sheet." However, this window remains limited: "that window will not remain open forever."

Additional figures (from search synthesis of the same source): China's official measure of government debt is 61% of GDP in 2024, but this excludes LGFV (local government financing vehicle) debt of about 50% of GDP and general government funds of about 14%. Corporate debt-to-GDP is higher in China now than in Japan in 1990.

The core warning is that postponing asset revaluation will eventually force losses onto the economy through either controlled policy mechanisms or disruptive market mechanisms.
