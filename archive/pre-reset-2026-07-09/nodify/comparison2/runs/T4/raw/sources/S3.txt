## Economic Diversification
The article emphasizes China's broader economic base: "China has seized the development opportunities of new energy and the Internet, and has performed well in the fields such as digital economy and new energy vehicles." This contrasts with Japan's real estate overdependence.

## Export Competitiveness
China maintains global trade strength: "China's global share of exports is still above 14 percent, leading other economies." A weaker yuan supports export resilience despite domestic headwinds.

## Government Control & Policy Effectiveness
State control over finance is cited as critical: "the Chinese government has full control over its banking system" (per Desmond Lachman), making systemic banking crises unlikely and enabling coordinated policy responses.

## Currency Management
Unlike Japan's sharp yen appreciation, "China...carefully manages its currency, ensuring the yuan trades in a tight range versus other major currencies."

## Asset Price Decline Severity
The article notes China's real estate correction has been less severe: "the fall in asset prices has been far less catastrophic than that in Japan in 1990 and the United States in 2008."

## Market Scale & Strategic Planning
The Chinese economy benefits from "a huge domestic market" and is "guided by a national strategy and long-term plans for manufacturing development," providing inherent stability.
