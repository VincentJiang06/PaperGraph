**Abstract Summary:**
The study examined Seattle's minimum wage increases from $9.47 to $11 (2015) and $13 (2016). Researchers analyzed employment across sectors paying below specified wage thresholds.

**Key Verbatim Findings on Second Phase-In ($13):**
1. Hours effect: "the second wage increase to $13 reduced hours worked in low-wage jobs by 6-7 percent"
2. Wage effect: "hourly wages in such jobs increased by 3 percent"
3. Overall impact: "total payroll for such jobs decreased, implying that the Ordinance lowered the amount paid to workers in low-wage jobs by an average of $74 per month per job in 2016"

**First Phase-In Note:**
The authors note they found "more modest effects" from the initial $9-to-$11 increase and "an effect of zero when analyzing employment in the restaurant industry at all wage levels."
