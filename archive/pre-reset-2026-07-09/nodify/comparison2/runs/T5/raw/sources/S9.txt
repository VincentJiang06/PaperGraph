**Disemployment Variation:**
"disemployment effects were greater in industries where passing the wage costs to consumers is more difficult"

The researchers also note that employment elasticities remained negative but modest even four years post-reform.

(Additional context from earlier search snippet on this paper: the paper analyzes a large (~60%) and persistent increase in Hungary's minimum wage instituted in 2001, where the minimum-to-median wage ratio rose from about 35% before 2000 to about 55% two years later; effects were stronger in low-wage segments and depressed regions where the minimum wage "bites" deeper into the wage distribution.)
