## Abstract Summary
The researchers developed a novel bunching estimator comparing excess jobs at the new minimum wage to missing jobs below it. They analyzed 138 state minimum wage changes between 1979-2016.

## Key Findings on Overall Employment Effect
"the overall number of low-wage jobs remained essentially unchanged over five years following the increase"

This suggests minimal aggregate job loss from minimum wage increases.

## Findings on Wage "Bite" Effects
The paper states: "We also find no evidence of disemployment when we consider higher levels of minimum wages. However, we do find some evidence of reduced employment in tradable sectors."

This indicates employment impacts varied by sector rather than showing clear patterns related to the minimum wage's relative magnitude (its "bite" as a ratio to median wages). The researchers noted wage spillover effects amplified earnings gains at the bottom of the wage distribution alongside stable overall employment.

(Additional context from search results, Department of Labor CLEAR summary and press coverage: the paper uses data through 2016 and separately reports heterogeneity by the "bite" of the minimum wage, i.e., the minimum-to-median wage ratio, finding little indication of job losses for minimum-wage events reaching up to roughly 59% of the local median wage.)
