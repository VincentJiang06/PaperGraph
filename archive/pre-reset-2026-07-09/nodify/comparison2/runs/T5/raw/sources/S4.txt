## Abstract
The study examined Seattle's minimum wage ordinance using administrative employment data from Washington state. Researchers tracked individuals in low-wage jobs before wage increases and compared outcomes using matched counterfactuals.

## Key Findings

**Wage and Earnings Effects:**
The researchers "attribute significant hourly wage increases and hours reductions to the policy." Overall, the wage increase "raised earnings by an average of $8-$12 per week."

**Distributional Impacts:**
Crucially, "The entirety of these gains accrued to workers with above-median experience at baseline; less-experienced workers saw no significant change to weekly pay."

**Employment Stability:**
The ordinance was "associate[d] with an 8% reduction in job turnover rates as well as a significant reduction in the rate of new entries into the workforce."

**Labor Market Adjustment:**
Approximately one-quarter of earnings gains came from "experienced workers making up for lost hours in Seattle with work outside the city limits."

The study indicates heterogeneous effects, with experienced workers benefiting while less-experienced workers faced hours cuts that offset wage gains.
