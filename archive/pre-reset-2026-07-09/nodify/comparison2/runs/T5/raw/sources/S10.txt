Extracted content:

**Research Focus:**
The study examines how minimum wage increases affect employment in jobs susceptible to automation, particularly for low-skilled workers.

**Key Finding:**
"increasing the minimum wage decreases significantly the share of automatable employment held by low-skilled workers"

**Additional Impact:**
The research shows that wage increases "increases the likelihood that low-skilled workers in automatable jobs become nonemployed or employed in worse jobs."

**Vulnerable Groups:**
The authors identify particularly concerning effects for "older, low-skilled workers in manufacturing," noting these demographic groups face substantive adverse employment changes due to automation triggered by minimum wage increases.

**Publication Details:**
Working Paper 23667, August 2017 (revised January 2018); published in Labour Economics, 2018; Authors: Grace Lordan and David Neumark.

(Additional context from search results: based on CPS data 1980-2015, a $1 minimum wage increase is associated with roughly a 0.43 percentage point decline in the share of automatable jobs held by low-skilled workers overall, and a 0.94 percentage point decline for workers 25 and younger.)
