## Abstract Summary
The researchers investigate why minimum wage employment effects are typically near zero, proposing monopsony as an explanation. They examine a retail sector using U.S. labor market concentration data with fine spatial detail.

## Key Findings on Employment Effects by Concentration

Finding 1: "more concentrated labor markets – where wages are more likely to be below marginal productivity – experience significantly more positive employment effects from the minimum wage"

Finding 2: Employment effects become progressively less negative as concentration increases: "minimum wage-induced employment changes become less negative as labor concentration increases, and are even estimated to be positive in the most highly concentrated markets"

Finding 3: The opposite pattern appears in competitive markets: "increases in the minimum wage are found to significantly decrease employment of workers in low concentration markets"

## Implication
The authors conclude these results provide "direct empirical evidence supporting the monopsony model" and suggest aggregate minimum wage employment estimates may conceal important variation across different labor market concentration levels.
