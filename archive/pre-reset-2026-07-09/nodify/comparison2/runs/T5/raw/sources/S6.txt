**Abstract Summary:**
The authors review employment effects literature on minimum wages from the 1990s onward. They conclude that despite claims otherwise, recent research generally supports the traditional economic view that minimum wages reduce low-wage employment.

**Key Verbatim Findings:**

On the overall evidence:
"A sizable majority of the studies surveyed in this monograph give a relatively consistent indication of negative employment effects of minimum wages."

On the most credible research:
"Among the papers we view as providing the most credible evidence, almost all point to negative employment effects, both for the United States as well as for many other countries."

On vulnerable workers:
"The studies that focus on the least-skilled groups provide relatively overwhelming evidence of stronger disemployment effects for these groups."

On positive employment effects:
"We see very few - if any - studies that provide convincing evidence of positive employment effects of minimum wages."
