Title: Are Minimum Wage Effects Greater in Low-Wage Areas?
Authors: Anna Godoey, Michael Reich
Published: IRLE / CWED, UC Berkeley working paper (2021); related published version in Industrial Relations.
URL: https://irle.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Are-Minimum-Wage-Effects-Greater-in-Low-Wage-Areas-1.pdf

Study description: examines 51 minimum wage events in about 750 counties across 45 states between 2004 and 2016, exploiting within-state county-level variation in local median wages so that the same statutory minimum wage produces a much higher "bite" (minimum-to-median wage ratio) in low-wage counties than in high-wage counties; uses event-study and difference-in-differences methods with ACS, QWI, and QCEW data.

Verbatim / closely-quoted key findings (as extracted):
In the counties with the highest relative bite, "relative minimum wage ratios reach as high as .82" (82 percent of the local median wage).
Even at this high bite, the study found positive wage effects (especially in high-impact counties) but "did not detect adverse effects on employment, weekly hours or annual weeks worked," including no negative employment effects among women, Black, and/or Hispanic workers.
Context: "previous research on recent state-level minimum wage policies did not extend beyond the $10 level, with the highest studied state-level ratio of the minimum wage to the median wage being 59 percent" -- i.e., this county-level design pushes the tested bite range well beyond the ~59% ceiling of prior state-level studies such as Cengiz et al. (S1).
