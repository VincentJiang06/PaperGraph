=== EXTRACTED CONTENT / VERBATIM QUOTES ===

Main Argument:
Despite predictions of massive AI-driven job displacement, actual evidence shows minimal labor market disruption two years after ChatGPT's launch.

Evidence of Weak Job-Loss Signal:
"Separation rates in AI-exposed occupations haven't budged. Job-finding rates for the unemployed track normally."
The Dallas Federal Reserve concluded: "data do not give much indication of widespread labor market disruption due to AI."
Weakness in fields like computer science predates ChatGPT: it "began in early 2022—months before ChatGPT launched—consistent with Fed tightening."

Alternative Explanation (selectivity, not destruction):
"employment among 20-to-24-year-olds in high AI-exposure occupations has slipped nearly a percentage point since late 2022," but "employers aren't firing people. Rather, they're raising the bar for new hires." Paradoxically, those hired for AI-exposed roles "are earning higher starting salaries than their predecessors."

Calibrated Conclusion:
"The job apocalypse may yet arrive. For now, it's just early—which, in forecasting, is another way of saying wrong."
