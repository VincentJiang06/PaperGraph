Source A: St. Louis Fed / Harvard survey research (Bick, Blandin, Deming et al.), "The Rapid Adoption of Generative AI" (NBER WP 32966) and Harvard Gazette coverage (Oct 2024).
URL: https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2024/10/generative-ai-embraced-faster-than-internet-pcs/

Verbatim: as of August 2024 (about 21 months after ChatGPT's Nov-2022 launch), "nearly 40 percent of U.S. adults aged 18-64 had used generative AI" (28% at work, 33% outside work). Historical comparators at similar post-launch points: the internet reached "20 percent after two years," and personal computers reached "20 percent after three years, the earliest researchers could measure." I.e., generative AI's CONSUMER/individual USAGE adoption rate is running roughly 2x the level the internet and PCs had reached at a comparable point in their diffusion — a genuinely fast, historically unusual adoption pace.

Source B: US Census Bureau, Business Trends and Outlook Survey (BTOS), year-end 2025 data; Federal Reserve, "Monitoring AI Adoption in the U.S. Economy" (FEDS Notes, April 2026).
URL: https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/monitoring-ai-adoption-in-the-u-s-economy-20260403.html ; https://www.census.gov/library/stories/2026/05/ai-use-businesses.html

Verbatim/key figures: "about 18 percent of firms have adopted AI as of year-end 2025" (firm-weighted; small firms drag this down), but on an employment-weighted basis roughly 78% of the labor force works at firms with SOME AI adoption in at least one business function (large firms adopt disproportionately). Individual-level generative AI USE for work purposes: roughly 41% of workers. BTOS overall usage hovered 17-20% through Dec 2025-May 2026, with 20-23% of businesses expecting to adopt within six months. Prior to late 2025 the adoption rate had been growing about 68% annually, though growth decelerated in Q2 2025 — i.e. still a fast-growing base, but the growth rate itself is already showing signs of deceleration less than 3 years after launch, unlike the multi-decade monotonic ramp historically seen for electrification.

Source C: Big Tech AI capital expenditure trend (multiple market-data aggregators, cross-checked).
Big Tech (Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, Oracle) AI-related capex nearly tripled from about $162B (2022) to roughly $405-448B (2025), and the five largest cloud/AI infrastructure providers have guided a further near-doubling to $660-690B for 2026 — i.e. capex is not just large but still ACCELERATING three years into the cycle, consistent with the "front-loaded intangible/infrastructure investment precedes measured output gains" mechanism of the Productivity J-Curve (DOC-0012).

Relevance: These are exactly the kind of forward-looking, currently-observable indicators N-0015 asks for to stress-test the falsifiability of the "lag" hypothesis. The reading is genuinely mixed and does NOT unambiguously support either "definitely still early / wait for it" or "the lag story is already failing": (1) individual USAGE adoption is diffusing unusually fast (faster than internet/PC at comparable points), which cuts AGAINST a multi-decade electrification-style lag; (2) but FIRM-level structural adoption (workflow redesign, the kind of intangible investment the J-Curve theory says actually drives the productivity/employment payoff) remains narrow (~18-20% of firms) and its growth rate is already decelerating; (3) capital investment (capex) is still accelerating rapidly, which is consistent with the economy still being in the early, investment-heavy phase of a J-curve rather than the payoff phase. On balance this is not (yet) a clean falsification of the lag hypothesis, but it does narrow it: if usage is already fast and firm adoption growth is already decelerating within 3 years, a multi-decade (electrification-style) lag looks less likely than a shorter (single-digit-years, IT-productivity-paradox-style ~15-20 year) or even faster lag — an update AGAINST the longest historical precedent, not a confirmation of "any length of lag is possible."
