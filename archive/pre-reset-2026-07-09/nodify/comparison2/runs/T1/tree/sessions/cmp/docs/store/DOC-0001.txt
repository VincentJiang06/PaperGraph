Source: "Generative AI at Work" — Erik Brynjolfsson, Danielle Li, Lindsey Raymond. NBER Working Paper No. 31161 (2023); published in The Quarterly Journal of Economics, 2025.
URL: https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161

Verbatim / near-verbatim key sentences (as extracted from NBER page and QJE publication summary):

"We study the staggered introduction of a generative AI-based conversational assistant using data from 5,179 customer support agents."

"Access to the tool increases productivity, as measured by issues resolved per hour, by 14% on average, including a 34% improvement for novice and low-skilled workers but with minimal impact on experienced and highly skilled workers."

"We also find that the AI model disseminates the best practices of more able workers and helps newer workers move down the experience curve."

"AI assistance improves customer sentiment, increases employee retention, and may lead to worker learning."

Methodology: staggered rollout of a generative-AI-based conversational assistant across a large customer support operation, comparing agents before/after and treated/untreated in a difference-in-differences design across 5,179 agents.

Relevance: This is one of the most-cited firm-level causal (quasi-experimental) studies of generative AI's task-level effect on white-collar service work. It quantifies task-level productivity gain, not job destruction — the study measures issues resolved per hour, not headcount change. The effect is heavily skewed toward low-skill/novice workers (34%) vs near-zero for experienced workers, suggesting AI's task-level effect compresses skill dispersion rather than uniformly displacing labor.
