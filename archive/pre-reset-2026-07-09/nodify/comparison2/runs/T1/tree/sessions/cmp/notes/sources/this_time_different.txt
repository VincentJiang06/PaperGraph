Argument (paraphrased): prior waves of automation typically picked off tasks one at a time within an occupation, leaving the occupation intact as a "coordination shell" of judgment/oversight tasks around the remaining human work (this is exactly the ATM/bank-teller pattern in DOC-0009). Agentic AI is qualitatively different because it is built to take a general instruction and autonomously plan + execute a full multi-step workflow end-to-end, potentially removing the need for the human "coordination shell" itself, not just individual sub-tasks within it.

Source C: general economist commentary (search-aggregated), noting genuine, unresolved disagreement: Acemoglu (MIT) remains comparatively cautious even about the agentic phase, warning primarily that it may favor already-skilled workers and widen inequality rather than necessarily causing mass net job loss; other commentators argue the ability of agents to "enter into economic relationships and transactions" autonomously (not just execute isolated tasks) is a structurally new capability without a clean historical precedent.

Relevance: This is the direct evidentiary basis for evaluating N-0013 ("this time is different"). The strongest, most specific version of the argument is not "AI is powerful" in the abstract, but the concrete structural claim that AI AGENTS (as opposed to earlier single-task generative-AI tools) are designed to automate the coordinating/integrating function of a job, not just its sub-tasks — which is precisely the condition under which the Bessen ATM mechanism (DOC-0009) is documented to break down (cf. the post-2010 mobile-banking case). The IMF's own 40%/60% exposure estimates explicitly split roughly 50/50 between complementary and substitutive outcomes, which is itself agnostic on the "this time is different" question — it is compatible with either a historically-normal mixed outcome or an accelerating substitutive share as agents mature.
