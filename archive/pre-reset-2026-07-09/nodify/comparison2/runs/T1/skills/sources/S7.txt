Verbatim key quotes:

"The US unemployment rate dropped to 4.2% in June 2026, down from 4.3% in May and below expectations."

Historical reference points shown: April 2020 = 14.8% (pandemic peak); May 2026 = 4.3%; June 2026 = 4.2%.

Additional detail: Number of unemployed persons = 7.09 million; total employment = 162.26 million; labor force participation rate = 61.5% ("lowest since March 2021"); U-6 (broader underemployment) rate = 7.9%.

Note in fetched summary: "The decline occurred despite workforce contraction, as many people left the labor force rather than finding new employment."

Notes: The headline unemployment rate (4.2%) looks mild/stable by historical standards, but the falling labor-force participation rate (lowest since March 2021) alongside it suggests part of the "mild" headline number may reflect people exiting the labor force rather than being counted unemployed — a measurement caveat relevant to the causal-identification question.
